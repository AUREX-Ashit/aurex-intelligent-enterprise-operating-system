## SECTION 1: Purpose & Scope

SECTION 1 — Purpose, Vision & Scope

1.1 Purpose

This document defines the canonical Presentation Architecture of the Enterprise Intelligence Fabric.

It establishes the principles governing how Enterprise Intelligence is presented consistently across every interaction surface, ensuring that every presentation experience is evidence-driven, explainable, configurable, composable, and adaptable.

Presentation Architecture is independent of business domains, industries, reporting frameworks, user interfaces, AI providers, and implementation technologies. It provides a timeless architectural foundation capable of evolving with future enterprise operating models and interaction paradigms.

1.2 Vision

The Enterprise Intelligence Platform exists to help organizations continuously understand themselves, understand the external world, and make better business decisions.

The platform is founded on four architectural concepts:

Enterprise Understanding — a continuously evolving digital understanding of the enterprise, including its structure, operations, capabilities, relationships, resources, obligations, performance, and institutional knowledge.

External World Intelligence — continuously evolving intelligence describing markets, regulations, customers, suppliers, competitors, technologies, geopolitical events, macroeconomic conditions, and every other external factor influencing the enterprise.

Enterprise Intelligence — intelligence generated through the continuous interpretation of Enterprise Understanding within the context of External World Intelligence.

Enterprise Intelligence Fabric — the unified platform that continuously acquires, interprets, enriches, governs, presents, and exchanges Enterprise Intelligence across the organization.

Presentation Architecture exists to make this intelligence understandable, explainable, actionable, and continuously accessible.

1.3 Scope

This document governs every presentation experience delivered by the Enterprise Intelligence Fabric.

Presentation experiences include, but are not limited to:

Workspaces
Business Activities
Enterprise Objects
Dashboards
Executive Views
Operational Views
Search Experiences
AI Conversations
Collaboration Experiences
Evidence Exploration
Decision Support
Reports
Exchange Interfaces
Future interaction modalities

The architecture intentionally avoids prescribing visual design, user interface technology, or implementation frameworks. Instead, it defines the universal presentation principles that remain valid regardless of how users interact with the platform.

1.4 Enterprise Presentation Philosophy

Presentation is not the source of intelligence.

Presentation is the mechanism through which Enterprise Intelligence becomes understandable, explainable, trustworthy, collaborative, and actionable.

Presentation must therefore:

Consume intelligence rather than create it.
Explain intelligence rather than obscure it.
Guide decisions rather than automate responsibility.
Present evidence before conclusions.
Adapt to enterprise context without fragmenting the platform.
Minimize cognitive effort while maximizing business understanding.

Artificial Intelligence is an embedded capability throughout the Presentation Architecture. It is never treated as a separate destination, feature, or application. Every presentation surface may intelligently assist users while remaining transparent, explainable, and governed by human oversight.

Similarly, reports are presentation outcomes rather than presentation objectives. Every report represents one possible rendering of Enterprise Intelligence rather than a separate business process.

1.5 Enterprise Intelligence Presentation Model

Enterprise Intelligence flows continuously through four logical stages:

Enterprise Understanding
            ↓
External World Intelligence
            ↓
Enterprise Intelligence
            ↓
Presentation Experiences

Presentation experiences consume Enterprise Intelligence through a unified presentation architecture, ensuring that every user, regardless of role, receives information appropriate to their responsibilities while maintaining a single, consistent understanding of the enterprise.

Different users may experience different presentations, but they always consume the same underlying Enterprise Intelligence.

1.6 Foundational Presentation Principles

The Presentation Architecture is governed by the following immutable principles:

Presentation consumes Enterprise Intelligence; it never owns Enterprise Intelligence.
Presentation is evidence-first, never opinion-first.
Every conclusion is explainable.
Every insight is traceable.
Every presentation experience is metadata-driven.
Every experience is composable.
Every experience is configurable.
Every experience adapts without requiring platform customization.
Artificial Intelligence augments human judgment; it never replaces enterprise accountability.
Human attention is the platform's most valuable resource and shall never be wasted.

These principles establish the architectural foundation upon which every subsequent section of this document is built.

The purpose of Presentation Architecture is not to display information.

Its purpose is to create enterprise understanding.

Knowledge becomes Enterprise Intelligence only when interpreted in relation to both the enterprise and the continuously changing external world.

Every presentation experience must therefore improve understanding rather than merely increase information density.

Presentation Architecture continuously incorporates relevant intelligence originating outside the enterprise, including:

Markets

External World Intelligence continuously changes the interpretation of Enterprise Understanding.

Enterprise Intelligence is not stored.

It is continuously created.

Every insight presented by the platform is the result of interpreting Enterprise Understanding within the context of External World Intelligence.

As either understanding evolves, Enterprise Intelligence evolves.

Presentation Architecture must therefore support continuous interpretation rather than static reporting.

2.5 Presentation Creates Understanding, Not Information

Presentation should reduce complexity rather than increase it.

Every presentation experience should:

provide context before detail
present evidence before conclusions
explain reasoning before recommendations
expose confidence before certainty
reveal consequences before actions

Presentation succeeds when users understand more while processing less information.

2.6 AI is an Embedded Capability

Artificial Intelligence is embedded throughout the Enterprise Intelligence Fabric.

It is not a destination, application, assistant, or separate user experience.

AI continuously assists in:

discovery
interpretation
summarization
explanation
prediction
recommendation
collaboration
personalization

Users interact with Enterprise Intelligence.

They do not interact with "the AI."

Whenever AI contributes to Enterprise Intelligence, its reasoning, evidence, assumptions, confidence, and limitations must remain transparent and explainable.

2.7 Reports are Outcomes, Not Objectives

Reports are one possible rendering of Enterprise Intelligence.

They are not the purpose of the platform.

Every report should be generated from continuously evolving Enterprise Intelligence rather than independently authored.

Reports therefore become reusable presentation outcomes rather than isolated deliverables.

2.8 One Enterprise Intelligence, Infinite Presentations

The Enterprise Intelligence Fabric maintains a single Enterprise Intelligence model.

Different presentation experiences may organize, summarize, personalize, visualize, or explain that intelligence differently according to user responsibilities, enterprise context, and business objectives.

Presentation diversity must never create multiple versions of enterprise truth.

Every presentation experience consumes the same Enterprise Intelligence while presenting it appropriately for its intended audience.

## SECTION 3: Guided Completion & the Question Engine

This section defines how a screen behaves when the platform does not yet have an answer — the single most important interaction sequence in Aurex, and the one most exposed in v1.0 (only 4 of the 6 required steps were represented).

**SD-001-004: The Six-Step Resolution Sequence**

Before any screen asks a human a question, the platform must attempt, in order: **Extract** (from an uploaded or connected document) → **Retrieve** (from a prior period or a related object already on file) → **Infer** (from correlated data the platform already holds) → **Confirm** (surface the inferred value for one-click human confirmation rather than re-entry) → **Route** (send the unresolved item to the person best positioned to know, per SD-001-006) → **Ask** (only if no better-positioned owner exists or is reachable). A screen that jumps straight from "not found" to "please enter" without visibly attempting the first four steps is non-compliant.

**SD-001-005: Ask Only What Cannot Be Found**

Manual entry is the last-resort step in SD-001-004, never the first. Every Guided Completion prompt must show what the platform already tried before asking.

**SD-001-006: Route to the Person Closest to the Truth**

When an item cannot be resolved by Extract, Retrieve, Infer, or Confirm, the platform identifies the person most likely to know the answer — by role, ownership record, or prior authorship of the related object — and routes the item to them by default, rather than to whichever user happens to be viewing the screen. The current user is asked directly only when no better-positioned owner exists, is unavailable, or explicitly declines. Every routed item names its target owner on-screen; an unrouted, unowned gap is a design defect, not an acceptable end state (see L8, Section 9).

**SD-001-007: The Question Engine Is the Only Path to a Human Answer**

Every "Ask" step in SD-001-004 is issued by the Question Engine, not authored ad hoc by a screen designer. The Question Engine determines the minimum question needed to close a specific gap, frames it as a Business Activity (SD-001-009), and records the answer as evidence with full provenance (who answered, when, against what gap). No screen may present a raw, un-routed, un-provenanced text box as a substitute for a Question Engine prompt.

**SD-001-008: One Primary Question Per Screen**

A Guided Completion screen exposes exactly one primary question at a time. Supporting context (evidence already found, related fields, prior answers) may surround it, but the user is never asked to resolve multiple unrelated gaps in a single view. This protects executive and operational attention alike and is non-negotiable regardless of how many gaps exist in the underlying activity.

**SD-001-009: Business Activities Replace Questionnaires**

The platform exposes named business activities ("Cost of Delivery," "Supplier Risk Assessment," "Board Governance Review") rather than numbered question lists. An activity states what it improves and how long it takes before the user starts it. *Example: "Cost of Delivery — 10 inputs, 3 minutes, improves IFRS Readiness, Annual Report, and Resilience P&L."*

## SECTION 4: Evidence, Confidence & Trust Presentation

**SD-001-010: Confidence Is Always Visible, Never Buried**

Every discovered or inferred value on screen carries a visible confidence indicator. A user should never need to click to discover that a number is low-confidence — they may click to see *why*, but the confidence level itself is ambient, not hidden.

**SD-001-011: The Confidence Score Is a Disclosed Formula, Not a Black Box**

Confidence is computed from four named, weighted components — source reliability, data freshness, corroboration across independent sources, and completeness of required metadata — combined as: `(Reliability × 0.40) + (Freshness × 0.25) + (Corroboration × 0.20) + (Completeness × 0.15)`. Screens that display a confidence score must make this formula (or the platform's current equivalent) reachable in one click. A confidence number with no disclosed method behind it violates L11 regardless of how accurate it is.

**SD-001-012: No Black-Box Conclusions**

Any AI-generated conclusion — not just a raw data value — must expose its reasoning chain on demand: which evidence it used, what it inferred versus what it found directly, and what it did not have. This is distinct from SD-001-010/011, which govern data confidence; this principle governs *conclusions*, which is where v1.0 was silent.

**SD-001-013: Trust Thresholds Are Delegated Authority, Not AI Decision-Making**

A configured auto-approval threshold (e.g., "auto-approve items above 95% confidence") is a pre-authorized delegation from a named human role, not the AI making a decision on its own initiative. Every auto-approved item must display, on demand, which role's delegated policy authorized the approval and when that policy was last confirmed by that role. This resolves the v1.0 tension between "AI may auto-approve" and "recommendation, never decision": the AI executes a standing human decision: it does not originate one. If a screen cannot show whose delegated policy authorized an auto-approval, that auto-approval must not be displayed as final — it must be shown as pending human ratification.

**SD-001-014: Trust Preferences and Risk Appetite Are a Single Configuration, Not Two**

v1.0 defined AI Trust Models and Risk Appetite Profiles as separate metadata blocks governing the same underlying decision (how confident must the platform be before acting without a human). SD-001 screens shall render these as one unified "Decision Confidence Model" per tenant, to prevent customers from setting contradictory values in two places. The underlying configuration mechanics belong to SD-002 (Appendix A); this principle governs only how the screen presents the unified model.

**SD-001-015: Evidence Is One Click Away, Always**

Every value's evidence panel — source document, page or record reference, extraction date, and named owner — is reachable in exactly one click from wherever that value appears, on any screen, at any depth of navigation.

## SECTION 5: Screen Composition Principles

*(Rated World Class in independent review — retained from v1.0 with only numbering and language-purge changes.)*

**SD-001-016: Screens Are Metadata, Not Code**

A screen is defined by a metadata record (`screen_code`, `screen_name`, `widgets[]`), not an application file. Adding, modifying, hiding, or reordering a screen's sections requires no code deployment — only a metadata change. This enables customer-specific, industry-specific, and role-specific screen variants without forking the codebase.

**SD-001-017: Widgets Are First-Class Metadata Objects**

Everything visible on a screen is a widget — coverage cards, evidence panels, timelines, assignment panels, guided completion cards, risk matrices, approval queues. Each widget is independently addressable metadata (`widget_code`, `widget_type`, `collapsible`, `movable`, `visible_by_default`) and customers may add, hide, collapse, pin, reorder, or build custom widgets without code changes.

**SD-001-018: Navigation Is Metadata**

Menu structure is never hardcoded. Two customers in different industries may see entirely different navigation trees generated from the same platform, driven by tenant-level navigation metadata.

**SD-001-019: Views Are Metadata**

A "CFO View," "Board View," or custom view is a named metadata configuration (department scope + widget set), not a separate application. The same underlying business object renders differently by view without duplicating data.

**SD-001-020: Context Panels Are Universal**

Every business screen provides a right-hand intelligence panel exposing evidence, timeline, assignments, history, confidence, approvals, and dependencies for whatever the user is looking at. A user should never need to ask "why," "who," "when," or "how" — the panel already answers it.

**SD-001-021: Progressive Disclosure — Understand First, Investigate Later**

Screens expose information in four levels: Summary → Details → Evidence → Audit History. A user sees the headline number first and drills down only if they choose to; they are never shown the audit trail before the answer.

**SD-001-022: Screen Personalization**

Users may pin, favorite, hide, and reorder widgets, and set a default screen and layout, without affecting any other user's view of the same underlying data.

**SD-001-023: Screen and Widget Versioning**

Screen definitions and individual widgets are independently version-controlled with effective dates and modification history, enabling rollback and historical reconstruction of what any user saw on any date. Widget version changes that alter a widget's expected data shape require a stated compatibility contract so that a widget update never silently corrupts a screen that consumes it.

**SD-001-024: Layout Templates Are Reusable Metadata**

The platform provides standard, reusable layout templates (Dashboard, Master-Detail, Guided Completion, Approval Workflow, Timeline, Evidence Review) to guarantee visual consistency across every domain without redesigning each screen from scratch.

**SD-001-025: Every Empty State Is Actionable**

A screen with no data never says "No data found." It states what is missing and offers the same resolution paths as SD-001-004: discover automatically, upload a document, or enter manually as a last resort.

**SD-001-026: Screen Performance Transparency**

Any in-progress operation (discovery running, AI extraction in progress, evidence processing, approval pending) is shown with a stated percentage and estimated completion time. No black-box waiting.

## SECTION 6: Enterprise DNA & Adaptive Experience

*(New in v2.0 — this section did not exist in v1.0; only its numbering slot, SD-001-43 through SD-001-56, was reserved.)*

Every enterprise operates differently — not just in vocabulary or org structure, but in how it makes decisions. This section defines how the same Aurex screens adapt their behavior to an enterprise's operating character without any code change. **The underlying DNA configuration data model belongs to SD-002** (Appendix A); this section governs only how screens consume and express it.

**SD-001-027: Enterprise DNA Is a Resolved Screen Input, Not a Screen-Level Setting**

A screen never hardcodes "if consensus-driven, show X." Instead, every screen reads a resolved Enterprise DNA profile (decision style, risk appetite, approval culture, management style, AI trust level) exactly as it reads any other piece of resolved configuration, and adjusts its behavior according to the rules in this section.

**SD-001-028: Decision Style Governs Approval Screen Density**

A consensus-driven enterprise's approval screens surface all named stakeholders and their individual sign-off status. A centralized/founder-led enterprise's approval screens surface a single approver with a fast-path action. Same screen template, different rendered density — driven by DNA, not by a redesign.

**SD-001-029: Risk Appetite Governs Escalation Visibility, Not Escalation Existence**

Every enterprise escalates; a Conservative-DNA enterprise sees escalation triggers earlier and more prominently (surfaced at 70% of threshold), while an Aggressive-DNA enterprise sees them only at the threshold itself. The escalation mechanism (defined in SD-003, Appendix B) is identical; only its screen-level visibility threshold changes.

**SD-001-030: Management Style Governs Default Progressive-Disclosure Depth**

An exception-based management style enterprise defaults every screen to Level 1 (Summary) with exceptions promoted to visibility automatically. A detail-oriented management style enterprise defaults to Level 2 (Details) with exceptions available on the same click path. This is a DNA-driven default only — SD-001-021's four levels remain available to every user regardless of DNA.

**SD-001-031: AI Trust Level Governs Guided Completion Framing, Not Guided Completion Logic**

A low-AI-trust-level enterprise's Guided Completion screens show every inference with its confidence and source before offering one-click confirm. A high-AI-trust-level enterprise's screens show a pre-confirmed value with a visible "undo" rather than a required confirm step. The underlying resolution sequence (SD-001-004) does not change — only how many clicks stand between inference and acceptance.

**SD-001-032: DNA Adaptation Is Fully Explainable**

Whenever a screen's behavior differs from the platform default because of a DNA setting, the screen must be able to state why on demand ("Approval shown as single sign-off because [Company]'s Enterprise DNA is configured as Centralized Decision Style"). DNA-driven variation is never invisible.

**SD-001-033: DNA Profiles Do Not Fork the Platform**

No DNA configuration produces a custom build, a customer-specific codebase, or a one-off screen. Every DNA-driven variation in this section is a rendering rule applied to the same universal screen metadata (Section 5). This is the mechanism by which Aurex claims "same platform, different enterprise behavior, no custom development" — and it is falsifiable: if a DNA setting ever requires a code branch, this principle has been violated.

## SECTION 7: Standard Screen Anatomy & the Action Center

*(Rated World Class in independent review. Retained from v1.0 with the Action Center now properly named and specified per L38, plus two new subsections closing gaps identified in review: Error & Conflict States, and Bulk Actions & Saved Views.)*

Every screen in Aurex follows the same structure regardless of domain, industry, customer, department, role, or device. A user learns Aurex once and uses it everywhere.

**Universal Screen Model:** Header & Context → Navigation & Filters → Primary Business Content (widgets, activities, business objects) → Right Intelligence Panel (evidence, assignments, history, confidence, Action Center) → Footer (version, audit, last updated).

**SD-001-034: Universal Header Structure** — every screen shows title, context, and breadcrumb in a fixed position.

**SD-001-035: Context Is Mandatory** — no screen renders without stating what entity, period, and scope it represents.

**SD-001-036: Primary Content Area** — hosts widgets, business activities, and business objects; never raw, unframed data tables as the primary experience.

**SD-001-037: Right Intelligence Panel** — evidence, assignments, history, confidence, and the Action Center live here, consistently, on every screen that has any of them.

**SD-001-038: Evidence Is Always One Click Away** — restated here structurally: the Right Intelligence Panel is the fixed location, every time.

**SD-001-039: Collaboration Is Native** — comments, mentions, and assignment changes happen in-context, without leaving the screen.

**SD-001-040: Audit Is Always Visible** — the footer states version, last modified by, and last modified date on every screen, unconditionally.

**SD-001-041: Empty States Must Be Actionable** — restated here structurally per SD-001-025.

**SD-001-042: Business Activities Over Forms** — the Primary Content Area frames work as named activities (SD-001-009), never bare forms.

**SD-001-043: The Action Center — Maximum Seven, Always Evidenced**

Every screen exposes at most seven actions in a single, consistently located Action Center component. Every action in the Action Center must cite the evidence or authority behind it (a pending approval names its approver and deadline; a suggested action names the data that triggered it). An eighth action is not added to the Action Center — it is either deferred, escalated, or delegated to a more specific screen. This is the direct, named implementation of L38, which v1.0 only approximated as a generically-named "Universal Action Bar" with no numeric cap and no evidence requirement.

**SD-001-044: Multiple View Modes** — every screen supports at minimum a summary and detail rendering of the same underlying data.

**SD-001-045: Consistent Footer Structure** — version, audit trail link, and last-updated timestamp, in the same position, on every screen.

**SD-001-046: Universal Screen Performance Indicators** — restated here structurally per SD-001-026.

**SD-001-047: Screen Accessibility Modes** — every screen anatomy element supports the accessibility modes defined in Section 10 without exception.

**SD-001-048: Screen Templates** — the anatomy above is instantiated through the layout templates defined in SD-001-024.

**SD-001-049: Error & Conflict States Are a Distinct, First-Class State**

An empty state (nothing found yet) and an error state (discovery failed, extraction failed, or two evidence sources disagree) are not the same experience and must not share a visual treatment. A conflict state names both disagreeing sources, their individual confidence scores, and offers resolution paths (accept one source, request a third source, route to the named owner per SD-001-006) — it never silently picks a winner.

**SD-001-050: Failed Discovery Is Reported, Not Hidden**

If an automated discovery attempt fails (a connector is down, a document is unreadable, a source returns no match), the screen states this explicitly and offers the next step in the SD-001-004 sequence rather than silently falling through to a blank field.

**SD-001-051: Bulk Actions Are a Universal List-View Capability**

Any screen presenting more than one row of the same object type supports select-many and apply-one-action-to-selection, consistent with the Action Center's seven-item cap and evidence requirement (SD-001-043). This closes a gap flagged in review: v1.0 had no bulk-action pattern despite it being standard in every competitor platform named in this document's governing brief.

**SD-001-052: Saved Views Are User- and Role-Configurable**

A user may save a filtered, sorted list configuration as a named view, and a role-level default saved view may be configured by an administrator without code change.

## SECTION 8: The Two-Layer Sacred 12

*(New in v2.0. Operationalizes L33, which v1.0 mentioned exactly once, in Section 1's scope statement, and never defined further.)*

**SD-001-053: The Two Layers Are Structurally Distinct Screen Populations**

Layer 1 (Operational Intelligence — Discover, Validate, Complete, Govern) is where evidence is gathered and confirmed. Layer 2 (Executive Cognition — the Sacred 12) is where that evidence is synthesized into the twelve executive-facing screens that a CEO, CFO, or Board member actually uses to make a decision. A Layer 1 screen never presents itself as executive-final; a Sacred 12 screen never asks the executive to perform discovery work.

**SD-001-054: The Sacred 12 Consume, They Do Not Discover**

No Sacred 12 screen contains a Guided Completion prompt, an Extract/Retrieve/Infer/Ask sequence, or a raw evidence-gathering activity. Every Sacred 12 screen renders only intelligence that Layer 1 has already resolved to a stated confidence level. If a Sacred 12 screen needs data Layer 1 hasn't resolved, the correct fix is a Layer 1 improvement — never a discovery widget bolted onto a Sacred 12 screen.

**SD-001-055: Every Sacred 12 Screen Obeys the Seven-Item and One-Question Laws Without Exception**

Because the Sacred 12 are executive cognition surfaces, SD-001-008 (one primary question per screen) and SD-001-043 (Action Center, maximum seven) are enforced most strictly here — an executive screen with eight action items or two competing headline questions has failed this document's core purpose regardless of how the rest of the platform behaves.

**SD-001-056: Calm Executive Seriousness Is a Rendering Requirement, Not a Style Guideline**

Sacred 12 screens use a visually calmer, lower-motion, lower-color-saturation treatment than Layer 1 operational screens. Urgency is communicated through stated financial or governance consequence (see SD-001-057), never through flashing indicators, red badges without context, or notification-style visual noise. This operationalizes L21 — v1.0 had no principle addressing screen tone at all.

**SD-001-057: Every Sacred 12 Insight States Its Business Consequence**

A number on a Sacred 12 screen is never presented alone. It is paired with the decision it should inform or the financial/governance consequence it represents, stated in one line, without requiring a click. *This is the screen-facing expression of L30; the underlying insight-to-consequence computation itself is an SD-002/intelligence-engine concern, correctly out of SD-001's scope — SD-001 governs only that the consequence, once computed, must be visible.*

## SECTION 10: Accessibility & Inclusivity Principles

**SD-001-059: Accessibility by Default** — WCAG-equivalent standards are the baseline configuration, not an opt-in mode.

**SD-001-060: Keyboard-First Navigation** — every action reachable by mouse is reachable by keyboard, with a visible focus order.

**SD-001-061: Screen Reader Compatibility** — every widget declares its accessible name, role, and state.

**SD-001-062: Color Is Never the Only Indicator** — status, confidence, and urgency are always paired with text or icon, never color alone.

**SD-001-063: Multiple Accessibility Modes** — high-contrast, reduced-motion, and large-text modes are tenant- and user-configurable.

**SD-001-064: Language Inclusivity** — accessibility standards apply identically across every supported language pack (Section 13).

**SD-001-065: Reading Complexity Preferences** — users may select a plain-language rendering of executive summaries where available.

**SD-001-066: Cultural Neutrality** — icons, metaphors, and color conventions are checked against unintended cultural meaning before global release.

**SD-001-067: Inclusive Collaboration Models** — commenting and assignment tools support asynchronous, timezone-neutral, and language-mixed collaboration.

**SD-001-068: Accessibility Testing Is a First-Class Release Gate** — no screen ships without an accessibility test pass, on the same footing as a functional test pass.

**SD-001-069: Human Dignity by Design** — no screen, even in an error or escalation state, frames a human user as the cause of failure; language always frames the system's next step, not the user's fault.

## SECTION 11: Mobile & Multi-Device Principles

**SD-001-070: Mobile Is a First-Class Platform** — not a reduced view; every Action Center item and evidence panel is fully functional on mobile.

**SD-001-071: Responsive Intelligence Design** — widget density adapts to viewport without hiding the confidence, evidence, or ownership data that Sections 4 and 9 require.

**SD-001-072: Context Preservation Across Devices** — a task started on desktop and continued on mobile resumes at the same point, same data, same confidence state.

**SD-001-073: Mobile-Optimized Actions** — the Action Center's seven-item cap (SD-001-043) is enforced identically on mobile; mobile never gets a longer action list because of screen real estate pressure — it gets a better-prioritized one.

**SD-001-074: Boardroom Display Modes** — a distinct, large-format, low-interaction rendering for shared displays (board meetings, town halls) that shows Sacred 12 content without exposing edit or discovery affordances.

**SD-001-075: Offline & Low-Bandwidth Support** — critical Sacred 12 screens degrade gracefully to cached last-known-good state with a visible staleness indicator, rather than failing to load.

**SD-001-076: Device-Aware Experiences** — the platform detects device class and adapts layout template (SD-001-024) automatically; no separate mobile app codebase.

## SECTION 12: Performance & Responsiveness Principles

**SD-001-077: Perceived Performance Matters as Much as Actual Performance** — a screen that shows meaningful content within 200ms, even before all data resolves, outperforms a screen that blocks fully until complete.

**SD-001-078: Every Long Task Shows Progress** — restated structurally per SD-001-026; no operation over one second renders without a progress indicator.

**SD-001-079: Progressive Loading** — above-the-fold content renders first; below-the-fold widgets load asynchronously.

**SD-001-080: Intelligent Caching** — previously resolved, high-confidence values render instantly from cache while a background refresh check runs silently, per LAW-13.

**SD-001-081: Incremental Refresh** — a screen updates the specific values that changed, not a full re-render, when new evidence arrives.

**SD-001-082: Transparent Background Processing** — any AI or discovery process running behind a screen is visible in the header (SD-001-034), never silently consuming resources with no user-visible state.

**SD-001-083: Performance Budgets Are Stated and Enforced**

Every screen template declares a maximum acceptable load time and a maximum widget count before performance is considered degraded; these budgets are tested at release, not discovered in production. This closes a v1.0 gap: performance principles existed with no stated numeric target to test against.

## SECTION 13: Internationalization & Localization Standards

**SD-001-084: Global-First Design** — no screen is designed for one locale and retrofitted; every screen is built against the localization model from day one.

**SD-001-085: Language Packs Are Metadata** — adding a supported language requires a language pack, not a code change.

**SD-001-086: Business Terminology Is Localizable** — regional business-unit terms (e.g., Crore, Lakh, Million, Billion) resolve per tenant locale.

**SD-001-087: Number Systems Are Configurable** — grouping and decimal conventions (1,00,00,000 vs. 10,000,000) render per locale.

**SD-001-088: Currency Representation Is Metadata** — multi-currency display, including conversion-date disclosure, is a locale setting, not a hardcoded assumption.

**SD-001-089: Date & Time Standards** — date format and timezone resolve per tenant and per user without ambiguity.

**SD-001-090: Right-to-Left (RTL) Support** — every screen layout template (SD-001-024) has a validated RTL rendering.

**SD-001-091: Regional Discovery Models** — the discovery step of SD-001-004 adapts its default evidence sources by jurisdiction (e.g., national company registries, market-specific regulatory filings) — the specific source list is an SD-002/intelligence-engine concern; SD-001 governs only that the screen must disclose which regional model is active.

**SD-001-092: Regional Reporting Standards** — report templates (SD-001-024) adapt to jurisdiction-specific statutory formats without a separate screen build per country.

**SD-001-093: Cultural Neutrality** — restated structurally per SD-001-066, applied specifically to localization content (translated idioms, imagery).

**SD-001-094: AI Localization** — AI-generated summaries and explanations (SD-001-012) render in the user's configured language, not just the underlying data.

**SD-001-095: Global Compliance Readiness** — screens supporting statutory disclosure (Layer 3) are built against the union of major jurisdictional requirements, not a single-market default, so that expansion to a new jurisdiction is a configuration exercise, not a redesign.

## SECTION 14: Extensibility & Marketplace Principles

**SD-001-096: Everything Extensible** — widgets, screens, activities, and report templates are all independently extensible by customers and partners without core platform changes.

**SD-001-097: Extension Registry** — every customer or partner extension is explicitly tracked (what was added, by whom, when), enabling upgrade-impact analysis.

**SD-001-098: Marketplace Categories** — Widget, Framework, Activity, and Template marketplaces are independently governed but share a single extension registry and approval model.

**SD-001-099: Safe Extension Principle** — an extension can add to a screen; it cannot alter global platform defaults (see SD-002 Appendix A, Override Without Pollution).

**SD-001-100: Marketplace Admission Criteria Are Explicit, Not Implied**

No widget, activity, or template enters a marketplace without passing the same accessibility (Section 10), performance budget (SD-001-083), and Action Center (SD-001-043) rules that apply to platform-native components. A marketplace listing states its confidence-scoring compatibility (Section 4) and its DNA-adaptation behavior (Section 6) before publication. This closes a v1.0 gap: marketplaces were described with no stated quality bar for admission.

**SD-001-101: Metadata Portability** — an extension's metadata is exportable and re-importable across tenants, enabling partner-built extensions to be sold without bespoke integration per customer.

**SD-001-102: Extension Approval Models** — a tenant administrator approves which marketplace extensions are enabled for their organization; approval is itself an auditable event per LAW-20.

## SECTION 15: Future-Proofing & Safe Evolution Principles

**SD-001-103: Metadata Over Code, Permanently** — this is a standing constraint on all future SD-001 additions, not just the current version.

**SD-001-104: AI Provider Independence** — no screen principle in this document assumes a specific AI provider; provider substitution must never require a screen redesign.

**SD-001-105: Framework Independence** — no screen hardcodes a specific reporting framework's structure; frameworks are configuration (SD-002 Appendix A).

**SD-001-106: Industry Independence** — no screen principle in this document assumes a specific industry; industry adaptation happens through DNA (Section 6) and configuration templates (SD-002 Appendix A).

**SD-001-107: Screen Independence** — a new screen can be added without modifying an existing screen's metadata.

**SD-001-108: Business Object Independence** — SD-001 principles never assume a specific business object schema; they consume whatever SD-002 defines.

**SD-001-109: Human Governance Principle** — no future capability may remove the human governance checkpoints established in Section 4, regardless of AI capability improvements.

**SD-001-110: Safe Evolution With a Named Deprecation Policy**

A deprecated field, widget, or screen template remains functional for a minimum of two major platform releases after deprecation is announced, with a visible in-product notice during that window, before removal. No release may remove a customer-facing element with fewer than two releases of prior notice. This closes a v1.0 gap: "backward compatibility" was promised with no stated process or duration.

## SECTION 16: AI-Native Enterprise Experience Framework

*(Added per `ADR-021`, Repository Owner Constitutional Design Workshop, 2026-08-07. This Section specializes this document's own existing Presentation Architecture authority; it does not introduce a new Layer 1 concern, and it modifies no principle in Sections 1–15.)*

**SD-001-111: AI-Native Experience — Definition and Scope**

AI-Native Experience is defined on two tiers. The outer, inclusive membership boundary is content-origin-based: any Enterprise Experience whose content originates, even partially, from an AI Runtime execution (`RTA-001 §13`) is AI-Native by definition. The inner boundary, determining what requires new Framework architecture, is pattern-novelty-based: only Experience whose interaction pattern itself — not merely its content — is structurally different from what this document's existing contracts already serve requires new work. Content inside the outer boundary but outside the inner one is AI-Native by definition and is already adequately served by Sections 4, 7, and 9 below.

**SD-001-112: Accountability Boundary**

AI-Native Experience gains no new constitutional status beyond this Section, never executes a Business Activity, and never owns canonical or durable state. Accountability remains fixed exclusively with Business Activities (`RTA-001 §13.2`/`§13.3`), inherited from `ADR-020`'s own precedent that Interaction, not Conversation, carries accountability — regardless of how many layers of presentation sit between a Business Activity and the AI Runtime output it presents.

**SD-001-113: Workspace Provides Context, Conversation Provides Continuity**

Workspace (`PE-001 §13.5`) provides Context; Conversation (`RTA-001 §13.15a`, `ADR-020`) provides Continuity — independent, non-conflicting properties. AI-Native Experience is embedded within Workspace navigation, never a new parallel navigation layer, consistent with SD-001-107 (Screen Independence) and every prior capability's own delivery precedent. Whether a specific capability's own Conversation is Workspace-bound or portable across Workspaces remains that capability's own CRB/ERB decision, per `PE-001 §13.5`'s own existing delegation — this Section does not override that delegation with a universal rule.

**SD-001-114: Composition Model**

AI-Native Experience is a Composition Framework: a small set of universally mandatory rules (Business Activity accountability, SD-001-112; Explainability, `LAW-26`; Runtime Event and Runtime Policy reuse with no parallel mechanism; Workspace embedding, SD-001-113), a set of mandatory-reuse component contracts applied wherever content shape matches and never re-invented (Progressive Disclosure, Section 3; the Evidence Panel and Confidence, Section 4), and a set of capability-owned extension points pulled in only where a specific capability's own content requires them, each remaining governed by its own existing document (AI Session Management, Agent Collaboration, Human Approval/Override, Recommendations, Enterprise Intelligence).

**SD-001-115: Reuse of Existing Contracts — No New Principle, No New Contract**

This Section owns interaction composition only. It introduces no new interaction principle and no new interaction contract. Progressive Disclosure (Section 3), the Evidence Panel, Confidence, and Trust Presentation (Section 4), Universal Design Laws (Section 9) including Explainability, and the Action Center (Section 7) are inherited unchanged by every AI-Native Experience this Section governs.

**SD-001-116: Capability-Layer Relationship and Adoption Model**

Adoption is content-triggered and capability-agnostic: the mandatory tier (SD-001-114) applies automatically, without exception, whenever — and only when — a capability's own Experience presents content originating from an AI Runtime execution, regardless of that capability's own `CAP-001` domain, and not restricted to D-005 (Enterprise Intelligence). This Section is not itself a `CAP-001` entry and owns no capability behavior. The governing chain is: Framework (this Section, capability-agnostic) → Capability (`CAP-001`, triggers the mandatory tier if AI-content is present) → CRB (capability-specific realization, `PE-001 §13.5`) → ERB (enterprise-specific variation) → Work Package (charters implementation) → Implementation (`IMP-001`-governed build) — each step consuming, never redefining, the step above it.

**SD-001-111: Enterprise Memory Principle** — nothing a customer has configured, extended, or answered is ever silently discarded across an upgrade; see LAW-24.

**SD-001-112: The Platform Adapts to the Enterprise** — restated as the closing principle of this document: every mechanism above exists so that Aurex bends to fit the customer, never the reverse.
