## 9. Model A / Model B — Independently Tested

`ADR-030 §5`: *"No single actor may perform both for the same authority"* — the prohibited act is named precisely: performing both Key 1 and Key 2, i.e., being the party who both ratifies the charter and makes the appointment decision.

`ADR-030 §6`: *"A Key-2 appointer must not be the same actor or session that performed Key 1... for that same authority"* — again precise: the restriction's grammatical subject is "a Key-2 appointer," the decision-making role, not the appointee/member.

`ADR-030 §8`: all four independence conditions are stated as requirements "a Key-2 appointer, for a given appointment, must" satisfy — condition 1 ("no prior involvement in Canonization") is textually a qualification on who may perform the appointing act, not on who may result from an appointing act performed by someone else.

**This Appointer's own conclusion, reached by direct reading of this text before reading either prior ROD's own conclusion on the point:** `ADR-030 §5`–`§8`, read for their literal and exact grammatical scope, restrict who may perform Key 2, not who may be selected by a valid, independent Key-2 act. "Model A" (permanent exclusion of any Key-1 participant from ever later holding the seat, regardless of who independently decides to appoint them) is not supported by this text as written — adopting it would require reading a restriction into `§5`–`§8` that their own chosen grammatical subject does not carry, which this Appointer is not authorized to do (`CLAUDE.md §18`/`§19.4` — an implementation or appointment actor does not invent stricter constitutional rules than the canonical text states). "Model B" (temporal separation — a Key-1 participant may not perform Key 2 or appoint themselves, but is not thereby barred from later occupying a seat a genuinely independent Key-2 appointer decides to give them) is the better-supported reading of the actual words used.

**This Appointer agrees with `ROD-C040-Key-1-Participant-Subsequent-Membership-Eligibility.md §12`–§13's own Model B conclusion — but reached it independently, by this same direct textual method, before treating that ROD's own conclusion as settled.** Agreement following independent verification is not the same failure mode as deference; this Appointer specifically checked whether it could construct a textually-grounded case for Model A that the prior ROD missed, and found none — every operative restriction in `§5`–`§8` is worded around the appointer/performer role, consistently, with no exception.
